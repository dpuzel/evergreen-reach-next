When someone nearby opens your Google listing, they are not only checking hours and photos. A lot of them tap the Facebook or Instagram button Google shows next to your name.

If that link still works, fine. They peek, and they come back. If it 404s, sends them to a page that has not posted since 2019, or opens someone else's shop, they do not usually leave a comment about it. They just decide the place is not being tended, and they look at the next result.

This is not a social media problem. It is a trust problem sitting on your Google porch. We already wrote about [the three misses we see most on Google](/notes/3-things-most-small-rural-businesses-get-wrong-on-google). A dead social link is a quieter version of the same thing: the listing looks abandoned, even when the shop is busy.

## What that link is actually doing

Google is not asking you to be an influencer. It is offering a shortcut.

That social button does three quiet jobs:

It proves the shop is the same shop. A stranger is trying to match your listing to a real place, not a name collision or an old card Google never cleaned up.

It proves somebody still shows up. A post from this month does more than a bio line that says "family owned since forever."

It proves the listing matches the rest of your life online. If [the photos look current](/notes/why-your-google-photos-matter-more-than-you-think) and the Facebook link is dead, the porch argues with itself.

You do not need a perfect feed. You need a door that opens to the right place.

## Dead looks like this

Dead is not only a broken URL. Dead is anything that makes a stranger feel like nobody has walked that path in a while.

- A Facebook or Instagram button that 404s
- A link to a page you deleted, renamed, or never owned
- A profile that still shows the old address, old phone, or a logo from a different paint job
- A feed whose last post is years old while the Google photos look fresh
- A link that opens another business with a similar name

Customer photos and reviews can still look alive while that one button quietly says the opposite. People trust the shortcut they can tap. If it fails once, they do not give you a second chance to explain.

## You do not need to "be on social"

If you do not use Facebook or Instagram, that is fine. The fix is not to start posting every day.

The fix is honesty on the listing:

- If the profile is real and yours, keep the link and make sure it opens.
- If the profile is abandoned, remove the link from Google until you are ready to tend it again.
- If you never had one, do not invent one. An empty social button is worse than no button.

A listing with no social links and clear hours, phone, and photos still looks tended. A listing with a dead social link looks like leftover internet.

## What to do this week

You do not need a content calendar. You need a two-minute pass.

1. Open your Google listing the way a stranger would, on a phone.
2. Tap every social button Google shows.
3. Write down anything that 404s, opens the wrong shop, or lands on a page that has not been touched in years.
4. In Google Business Profile, remove the bad links or replace them with the real profile URL you own today.
5. If the only current proof of life is on social, add one owner photo to the listing so the porch does not depend on a feed you barely use.

Then once a quarter, tap those buttons again. Links rot. Renames happen. Google keeps showing the old door until you change it.

## The listing should match the shop

Hours, phone, photos, and categories still have to tell the truth. A working social link will not save [a number that rings out](/notes/how-to-stop-missing-local-calls-and-leads). It will stop one more quiet reason for a neighbor to bounce.

If you want the whole walk, not just the social buttons, that is what a [Front Porch Report](/porch) is for. One of the lamps is whether the listing still matches the place. A [clean, simple presence](/notes/what-a-clean-simple-online-presence-looks-like) is the rest of the house matching that porch.

The goal is small. When someone nearby taps Facebook or Instagram from your Google listing, they should land on you, this year, at the right door. That is usually enough.
