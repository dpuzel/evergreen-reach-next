Many owners we talk to have a quiet list of things they mean to fix online. A better website. New photos. That social page they started a few years back. The list never gets shorter, because every item on it feels like it has to be done right or not at all.

So it sits.

Here's something we believe, and it shapes how we work: for a small local shop, good enough is a real standard. Not a shrug. A standard. It means the basics are true today, and somebody checks that they stay true.

## What does good enough actually mean?

It means a neighbor who looks you up can find you, reach you, and trust what they see. That's most of the job.

When we take a Front Porch walk, we look at six things, and we call them lamps. [What we look at on a Front Porch walk](/notes/what-we-look-at-on-a-front-porch-walk) goes through each one. Here's the short version, written as a good-enough list:

- **Found nearby.** You come up when someone searches for what you do in your town, and the pin sits on your door.
- **Call-ready.** The phone number is the same everywhere, and someone answers it or calls back.
- **Hours honest.** The hours on Google match the hours on the door, holidays included.
- **Photos alive.** There are a few recent pictures, including the outside, so people can find you.
- **Site not rotting.** The website loads, the links work, and nothing on it is out of date.
- **Name, address, phone.** Every place that lists you tells the same story.

None of those need a designer, a campaign, or a big budget. Most of them are an afternoon of checking and a few small fixes.

## Isn't perfect better?

Perfect is nice. It's also expensive, slow, and usually not what a customer is looking for.

A neighbor deciding whether to call you isn't grading your font. They want to know you're open, you're real, and you'll pick up. A plain site with the right hours and a working phone number does that. A beautiful site with last year's hours doesn't.

Chasing perfect has a hidden cost, too. When the bar is set that high, the small true fixes wait behind the big project. The hours stay wrong for months because the real website redo is coming soon.

## Why "kept true" is the hard part

Getting the basics right once isn't that hard. Keeping them right is where most listings slip.

Things drift. You change your winter hours and forget Google. A directory copies an old phone number. A stranger suggests an edit to your listing, and it can go through without you noticing. A photo from three years ago becomes the first thing people see. Nobody did anything wrong. Time just passed.

That's why we think of this as care, not a project. Good enough isn't a finish line you cross once. It's a standard you hold.

## How do I know my listing is good enough?

Take ten minutes on your phone, the way a customer would.

1. Search for what you do in your town. Do you show up?
2. Open your Google card. Do the hours match your door this week?
3. Tap the phone number. Does it ring the right place?
4. Look at the first few photos. Do they look like the shop today?
5. Open your website. Does it load, and is everything on it still true?
6. Look yourself up on one other site, like Yelp or MapQuest. Same name, address, and phone?

If all six check out, you're in good shape for now. Truly. You don't need to do more this month unless you want to.

If one or two are off, fix those and stop there. That's a good week.

## Holding the standard on a busy week

You don't need a system. You need a small habit.

Pick one day a month. The first Monday works. Run through the six checks with your coffee. Fix what's off, and put anything bigger on a list for a slower day. Before a holiday or a change of season, do it once more, since that's when hours tend to drift.

Ten minutes a month keeps a lot of quiet damage from piling up.

We won't tell you that good enough will put you at the top of the map or fill your phone. Nobody honest can promise that. What it does is make sure that when a neighbor does look, they find a shop that's open, real, and easy to reach. For a lot of small businesses, that's the whole point.

Do the ten-minute walk above. Write down any lamp that's off, and fix the easiest one before Friday.
