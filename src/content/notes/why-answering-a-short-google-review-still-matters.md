Somebody left you five stars and two words: "Great service!" That was three months ago, and it's still sitting there with nothing under it.

It's easy to skip. There isn't much to say back, and five stars feels like the job is already done. But a stranger scrolling your Google card doesn't only read the review. They read the quiet under it. A row of short reviews with no replies can look like nobody's minding the shop.

A reply fixes that, and it doesn't have to be long.

## Why reply to a review that barely says anything?

Because the reply is for the next person, too.

When someone is deciding between you and the place down the road, they look for signs that a real person is behind the listing. Honest hours help. [Photos that look like the place this month](/notes/why-your-google-photos-matter-more-than-you-think) help. A short "Thanks, Linda, good to see you again" under a review says someone is home and paying attention.

It also tells the person who wrote it that you noticed. People who take a minute to say something kind usually like hearing that it landed.

We won't tell you replies move you up the map. Nobody outside Google can promise that. What we can say is that a card with replies reads like a business that's open and cared for, and that's what a neighbor is checking for.

## Do I have to reply to every review?

No. You don't owe anybody a reply, and a busy week is a busy week.

If you're picking, start here:

- The short ones nobody answered. They're the easiest, and there are usually a few.
- Anything under four stars. A calm reply to a mixed review often tells a stranger more than the review itself.
- The newest ones. A reply from this month says more than one from last year.

If you only do one thing, answer each new review when it comes in. That alone keeps the card looking tended.

## What do I say to a two-word review?

Keep it short, warm, and true. A few that work:

- "Thank you! We appreciate you stopping in."
- "Thanks, Mike. That means a lot coming from a regular."
- "Appreciate it. Tell your neighbors we said hi."

A few things to skip:

- Don't paste the same line under every review. A wall of identical replies reads like a machine wrote them.
- Don't add details the reviewer didn't share, like what they bought, what they paid, or anything personal. Your reply is public.
- Don't stuff your town name or a list of services into it. It reads strangely, and it doesn't fool anyone.

## What about a bad review?

Same idea, slower. Wait until you're calm. Thank them, say you're sorry it went that way, and offer a way to talk it over off the page, like your phone number or the name of the person to ask for. Keep it to a few sentences, and don't argue the details in public. The person who really reads that reply is the next customer, and they're watching how you handle it.

If a review looks fake or breaks Google's rules, you can flag it for Google to review. Reply calmly anyway while you wait, since there's no telling how long it takes or whether it comes down.

## How do I reply to a review on Google?

Sign in with the Google account that manages your listing. Search your business name on Google or open it in Google Maps, then go to your reviews. Each one has a spot to reply, and your reply shows up under the review, marked as coming from the owner.

You can edit or delete a reply later if you want to change it.

## A five-minute habit

Pick a day. Monday morning with the coffee works. Open your reviews, answer anything new, and close it. Most weeks that's five minutes.

If you've got a backlog, don't try to clear a year of reviews in one sitting. Answer the five newest this week, then keep up as new ones come in. The habit matters more than the old ones.

## One thing to check this week

Look yourself up on your phone, the way a customer would, and scroll to your reviews. Find the newest one without a reply, and answer it in two sentences.

That's it. The next person who scrolls past will see someone's home.
