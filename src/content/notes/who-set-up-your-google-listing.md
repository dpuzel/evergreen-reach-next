Somebody set up your shop's Google listing. Maybe it was you. Maybe it was a nephew, an old web guy, or someone who hasn't worked there in years. Before you need to change your hours or fix a wrong phone number, it's worth checking one thing: can you still get in?

## The day you find out

Picture a made-up hardware store out past the edge of town. We'll call it Cedar Flat Hardware. The owner, Ruth, has run it for nineteen years. Her listing on Google has been there almost as long as the paint counter.

One Tuesday a customer mentions the phone number on Google rings a fax machine. Ruth figures it's a two-minute fix. She signs in to her Gmail, searches her shop's name, and waits for the little edit button.

There isn't one. Instead there's a line asking if she owns this business.

Turns out her son-in-law set the listing up years back, on an email he stopped using when he changed jobs. Nobody did anything wrong. It's just how a lot of listings got made: a helpful person, a spare account, and then life moved on.

The fix isn't hard. It just takes a few days, and it's a lot nicer to do on a slow week than on the morning the phone number is wrong.

## Step one: check which account holds the keys

Here's the quick check. It takes about five minutes.

1. Sign in to the Google account you think runs your listing. If you have a personal Gmail and a shop email, start with whichever one you'd guess.
2. Go to business.google.com, or search your shop's name and town on Google while you're signed in.
3. If you see your shop with tools to edit it, you're in. That's awesome. Skip down to "If you can get in."
4. If you see a "Claim this business" or "Own this business?" button instead, that doesn't only mean you're on the wrong account. Google says that button often means the profile isn't claimed yet. Sign out, switch to your other account, and look again. If the button is still there, click it. Two screens can come up. If the next screen is about managing or verifying the business, the listing is unclaimed and you can claim it. If you see a note that someone else may manage this Business Profile, someone already owns it. Use the Request Access steps further down.

Google itself points out that if you can't find the claim button, you might already be the owner. So the button is the tell. No button and edit tools showing means you're in. Button showing means the listing may be unclaimed, or someone else holds the keys.

## If you can get in

Good. Now make sure you stay in.

Google lets a listing have more than one owner and more than one manager, and each person uses their own Google account. That's the whole trick to never getting locked out again: don't let your shop's listing live on one account that one person might stop checking.

To see who has access:

1. Open your Business Profile while signed in.
2. Go to **More**, then **Business Profile settings**, then **People and access**.
3. Look at the list. Is everyone on it someone you still know and trust? Is your own name on it as an owner?

If the old web guy or a former employee is still listed and you don't need them anymore, an owner can remove them from that same page. If you'd like a backup person, like a spouse or a trusted manager, you can add them there too. Give them "Manager" if they only need to update hours, photos, and replies. Keep "Owner" for people who should be able to add or remove others. Once you're in, a good first job is checking your hours. Here's [what to do when Google shows inaccurate business hours](/notes/what-to-do-when-google-shows-the-wrong-hours).

One small heads-up from Google's own help page: someone who just became an owner has to wait 7 days before doing a few bigger things, like removing other owners, deleting or undeleting the profile, or transferring primary ownership. The same 7-day wait applies to newly added managers. So if you're cleaning house, add yourself first, then come back the next week to tidy up.

## If someone else holds the keys

This is Ruth's situation, and it's more common than you'd think.

The easiest path is the friendly one. If you know who set it up, ask them. If they can still sign in, they can add you as an owner from People and access in a couple of minutes, and you're done.

If you can't reach them, or don't know who it was, Google has a request process:

1. Go to business.google.com/add.
2. Enter your shop's name and address and pick it from the list.
3. You'll see a note that someone else may manage this profile. Select **Request Access**.
4. Fill out the form and submit it.

Google emails you a confirmation and emails the current owner. They have 3 days to answer. If they approve it, you're in. If they deny it, you can still suggest edits and appeal the decision. If they don't answer at all, you may get the option to claim the listing yourself through the link in your confirmation email. Google is honest that this claim option after the 3-day wait isn't always available.

One wrinkle: if you don't have a storefront and go to your customers instead, like a mobile groomer or a handyman, Google asks you to contact their support team and ask for "Transfer ownership of listing" rather than using the form.

Ruth asked her son-in-law. He dug up the old password, added her as owner that same evening, and she fixed the phone number before supper. Not every story is that tidy, but a lot of them are.

## One thing to do this week

Do the five-minute check. Sign in, find your listing, and see if you get edit tools or a claim button. Then write down which email address actually holds the keys and stick it somewhere you'll find it again, like the inside of the register drawer.

That's it. You don't need to change anything today. You just want to know the door opens before the day you need it to.
