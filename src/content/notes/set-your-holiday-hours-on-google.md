Thanksgiving is Thursday, November 26. Between now and New Year's, a lot of local shops close early, close for a day, or open late. Google doesn't know any of that unless you tell it.

So on the day before Thanksgiving, somebody searches your shop, sees your regular hours, and drives over. The door is locked. They don't call it a holiday. They call it a shop that can't keep its hours straight.

The fix takes about ten minutes, and you can do it all at once, today, for the whole season.

## Regular hours and special hours are two different things

Your regular hours are your normal week. Special hours are for a single day that's different: a holiday, an event, a snow day you saw coming.

Special hours sit on top of your regular hours for just that date. When the day passes, Google goes back to your normal week on its own. You don't have to remember to change anything back.

That's the whole trick. You're not rewriting your hours. You're adding a few dates.

## How to add them

On the Google account that manages your listing:

1. Search your business name on Google, or open your Business Profile.
2. Choose Edit profile, then Hours.
3. Next to Special hours, choose Edit.
4. Google may suggest upcoming holidays. Choose Review to confirm one, or choose Add a date.
5. For each date, either check Closed, or uncheck it and type the hours you'll be open.
6. Save.

Google's own help page lays these steps out: [How to set Special hours](https://support.google.com/business/answer/6303076). The buttons move around now and then, so if something looks a little different, look for the word Hours and go from there.

One small catch: special hours only show if your listing is set to open with main hours. If you've never touched that setting, it's probably fine, but take a look while you're in Hours.

## Which dates to add

Sit down with a calendar and go through the season once. For most shops around here, that's some mix of:

- Wednesday, November 25, if you close early before Thanksgiving
- Thursday, November 26, Thanksgiving
- Friday, November 27, Black Friday, if you're open different hours that day
- Saturday, November 28, Small Business Saturday, if you're open different hours that day
- Thursday, December 24, Christmas Eve
- Friday, December 25, Christmas
- Thursday, December 31, New Year's Eve
- Friday, January 1, New Year's Day

Here's one people skip: if you're open your normal hours on a holiday, add that day anyway with your normal hours. Google suggests this too. It tells a customer you actually looked, instead of leaving them to guess whether you forgot.

If you'll be closed a full week or more, say for a family trip after Christmas, special hours aren't the right tool. Google has a separate Temporarily closed setting for that. Just remember to switch it back when you're open again.

## Shorter days are a different fix

It's dark by five now. Plenty of shops shift their whole week for winter: close an hour earlier, or drop a weekday until spring.

That isn't a special hours job, because it isn't one day. It's your regular hours changing for a season. So change your regular hours, the same place under Edit profile, then Hours. Then put a note on your calendar for the spring to change them back.

And if your hours are different on the door, on your website, or on your Facebook page, fix those too. A customer who sees three different answers believes the worst one.

## A quick check when you're done

Search your shop name on your phone, the way a customer would. Tap on your hours. Look again around the 20th to make sure Thanksgiving shows the way you set it.
