Somewhere in your shop there's a stack of business cards. Maybe in a little holder by the register, maybe in a shoebox from the last print run. Grab one, pull up your shop on Google, and hold them side by side. Same name? Same phone number? Same address? That's the whole test, and it takes about a minute.

## Two numbers, one shop

Picture a made-up feed store on a county road. We'll call it Hollow Creek Feed. The owner, Marty, switched phone companies last spring and got a new number. He updated Google the same week, because his daughter told him to. Good move.

The cards were another story. He had a box of five hundred from the print shop, and they still had the old number on them. He kept handing them out anyway. At the fair, at the hay auction, tucked into the bag with every bale of shavings.

In November a woman who'd picked up a card at the fair called to ask about chicken feed for the winter. The old number was disconnected. She figured the store had closed and drove to the one in the next town.

Marty never knew she called. That's the quiet part. Nobody leaves a voicemail on a dead line.

Flip it around and it happens just as often. The card is right and Google is wrong, because someone typed the number years ago with two digits swapped and nobody looked since.

## The test, line by line

Lay the card on the counter. Open Google on your phone, search your shop's name, and look at your listing. Then go down the card one line at a time.

- **Name.** Is it the same name? Small stuff like "and" versus "&" is fine. "Hollow Creek Feed" on one and "Marty's Farm Supply" on the other is the kind of thing that makes a customer wonder if they found the right place.
- **Phone.** Read every digit out loud. Then call it from your cell. If it rings your counter, you're good.
- **Address.** Same street, same number, same suite if you have one. If you moved, this is the one most likely to be stale.
- **Website.** If your card has one, type it in exactly as printed. Does it load your site?
- **Hours.** Only if they're printed. Most cards skip them, and that's fine.

If everything matches, you're done. Put the card back and go sell some chicken feed.

## Which one to fix first

When they don't match, fix whichever one customers actually use. Most of the time that's Google.

Here's why. Your card only reaches people you've already met. They shook your hand. They know you're real. Google reaches strangers, the folks who've never been in and are deciding right now whether to drive over. A wrong number on Google costs you people you never got to meet.

So if Google is the one that's off, start there. If you have access to your listing, it's a short job:

1. Sign in to the Google account that manages your listing.
2. Search your shop's name on Google and find your listing.
3. Select **Edit profile**.
4. Fix the phone number, address, or whatever's wrong, then select **Save**.

Google reviews changes before they show up on your listing, so don't panic if it doesn't flip right away. Most updates show in about ten minutes. Some can take longer, up to 30 days, and that's still normal.

One heads-up on addresses: if you've actually moved to a new spot, Google asks you to verify the business again at the new address. That's normal. Plan for it instead of being surprised by it.

And if you search your shop and there's no Edit profile button for you at all, that's a different problem. Our note on [who set up your Google listing, and whether you can still get in](https://www.evergreen-reach.com/notes/who-set-up-your-google-listing) walks through that one.

## What about the cards?

You don't need to reprint over a small thing. Cards are cheap, but they're not free, and a box of five hundred is a box of five hundred.

If the card is still close and the number still rings, keep handing them out. If one digit is off and you're down to the last fifty, grab a pen, cross it out, and write the right number. Folks around here won't think less of you. Plenty will think more of you for caring enough to fix it.

When you do reorder, hold the proof next to your Google listing before you approve it. Same test, done once, before you've paid for five hundred of them.

## One thing to do this week

Do the test. One card, one phone, one minute. Name, phone, address, website.

If Google is wrong, fix Google first. If the card is wrong, decide whether it's a pen fix or a reorder. If everything matches, you just earned yourself a cup of coffee.

The week before Thanksgiving is a good time for it. Holiday markets, craft fairs, and church bazaars are when cards go out by the handful, and you'd like each one to ring.
