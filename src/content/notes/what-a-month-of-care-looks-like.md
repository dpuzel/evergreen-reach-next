People ask us a fair question: what do you actually do every month? It's a good thing to ask before you pay anyone for anything, so here's a plain answer.

We don't have a care client story to tell yet, so this is a made-up shop. Call it a small feed and tack store at the edge of town. Everything that goes wrong for it below is the kind of thing that goes wrong for local shops. What each plan covers is on our Plans page. This is the general picture, not a menu.

Each week ends with what you can do yourself. If you never hire anybody, you can still run this month on your own.

## Week 1: the look

Care starts the way everything with us starts: a free Front Porch walk. We look at the shop from the outside, the way a customer would. No logins, no passwords. We check the six lamps: found nearby, call-ready, hours honest, photos alive, site not rotting, and name, address, phone.

For our feed store, most of it looks fine. The Google card has the right hours. The photos are a little old, but they're real. Then we check the phone number, and the one on the Google card is a number the store stopped using a couple of years ago. MapQuest still shows it too.

That's the first thing we write down.

**On your own:** take the ten-minute phone walk from [Good enough is a real standard](/notes/good-enough-is-a-real-standard). Write down the one lamp that looks the most off.

## Week 2: the one fix

### How can someone fix my listing without my password?

They don't need it, and you shouldn't hand it over. You stay the owner. You decide what access to give, you can take it back any time, and the monthly note tells you what we changed.

For the feed store, the fix is the old phone number on its Google card. It's a small thing, but it's the kind that costs a call. Somebody finds the number, it rings nowhere, and they try the next store.

Why does a dead number hang around so long? Some directories copy their info from other directories, and some haven't been checked in years. One stale number can get passed down the line. So the fix is usually two parts: correct the listing, then keep an eye out that the old number hasn't crept back from somewhere else.

The MapQuest listing still has the old number. Listings like that sit outside the plain monthly list, so we'd tell the owner what's involved before touching it. Many directories also let the owner suggest the edit.

**On your own:** search your shop name and your town, and open the first two or three listings that aren't Google. Is the phone number the same everywhere? If one is wrong, look on that page for a link to suggest an edit or claim the listing. Many directories have one.

## Week 3: the watch

Most of care is quiet. Things drift on their own, so the job in week three is mostly watching:

- Hours, especially as a holiday gets close.
- Edits a stranger suggested on your Google listing.
- The map pin, and whether your service area still matches where you actually go.
- New reviews.

For the feed store, week three turns up two things. Thanksgiving is coming, and the Google hours don't say anything about it yet. And there's a new review from Saturday, three words long, with no reply.

The holiday hours we can set on Google. The review is a different story. We don't answer reviews for you, because your reply should sound like you. We point it out, and you take two minutes. [Why answering a short Google review still matters](/notes/why-answering-a-short-google-review-still-matters) covers what to say.

**On your own:** put a reminder on your calendar two weeks before each holiday to add special hours on Google. And check that Google is set to send you emails about your Business Profile, so you hear about suggested edits and new reviews when they happen.

## Week 4: the monthly note

At the end of the month, you get a short note in plain words. What we checked, what we changed, and anything we noticed that's yours to decide. No dashboard, no chart to decode.

For the feed store, it might read something like this:

> We checked your hours, phone, and address this month. We fixed the old phone number on Google. MapQuest still shows it, and we'll tell you what it takes to fix that one. Thanksgiving hours are set on Google. There's a review from Saturday waiting on your reply. Your photos are getting a little old, so if you snap a couple of the front this month, send them over.

Here's what the note won't say: that you moved up some number of spots, or that you got more calls because of us. We don't promise either one, and we won't dress a month up to look like we did.

**On your own:** keep a one-line note to yourself at the end of each month. What did you check, and what's still off? Over a year, that list tells you a lot.

## Can I just do this myself?

Yes. Everything above is in reach for an owner with a phone and a little time each week. That's why every section ends the way it does.

Care is for the months when you'd rather somebody else held the list. It's the same work, done by someone who remembers to look.

## One thing to check this week

Pick the week that sounds most like your shop right now and do just the "on your own" part. One week is plenty.
