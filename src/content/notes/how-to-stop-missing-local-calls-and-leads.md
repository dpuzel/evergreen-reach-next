A lot of owners think the problem is visibility. Sometimes it is. Just as often, people already found you. They tried to reach you. And the path broke.

Missed call. Wrong number. A form that lands in an inbox nobody opens. A "contact us" page with no hours and no expectation of when you will write back.

That is not a marketing problem. That is a leak.

## Find where the leak is

Spend one afternoon pretending you are a new customer.

- Search your name and what you do. If the [Google listing looks forgotten](/notes/3-things-most-small-rural-businesses-get-wrong-on-google), start there.
- Tap the call button on Google. Who picks up? How many rings?
- Try the number on your website. Same number, or a leftover one?
- Send a test message through the contact form. When does it arrive, and where?
- Check after-hours. What happens if someone calls at 7pm?

Write down every dead end. Most shops find at least one.

## Make the phone actually work

Your Google listing and your website should show one number that a real person answers during the hours you advertise.

If you are on a job and cannot pick up, that is normal. What is not normal is a voicemail box that is full, or a greeting from 2021 that says you will call right back when you will not.

A simple setup that works:

- One public number
- A clear voicemail: who you are, when you return calls, and that they should leave a name and what they need
- A habit of checking missed calls the same day, even if the reply is short
- Hours on Google that match when someone will actually answer

If evenings are when people call, say so. If you only return calls in the morning, say that too. People will wait for honesty. They will not wait for silence.

## Make the form worth filling out

A contact form is a promise. Someone took time. They told you their name and what they need.

That message has to land somewhere you will see it. Not a forgotten Gmail. Not a "sales@" address from a website you stopped using.

Keep the form short. Name, how to reach them, what they need, maybe the town. That is enough. Then reply like a neighbor, not a ticket system.

If you cannot get to a form the same day, say that on the page. "We read these on weekday mornings." People would rather know than guess.

## Stop making them hunt

The fastest way to lose a local lead is to hide.

- Put the phone number where a thumb can hit it on a phone
- Repeat it in the footer, not only in the hero
- Match the name, address, and phone across Google and the website
- If you serve a area instead of a storefront, say which towns

You do not need a funnel. You need a clear door.

## A weekly ten-minute check

Every week, not when you remember:

1. Call your own public number.
2. Send yourself a test form (or have someone else do it).
3. Look at missed calls and unreplied messages.
4. Confirm Google still shows the right number and hours.

That is the whole system. No dashboard required.

When someone nearby is ready to hire a local shop, they usually try once. If that try dies in a voicemail or a form that goes nowhere, they move on. Closing that leak is some of the highest-value work you can do, and it does not require a bigger website. It requires [a presence that is easy to reach](/notes/what-a-clean-simple-online-presence-looks-like), then someone who picks up or writes back.
